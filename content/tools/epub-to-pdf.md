## How do I convert an EPUB to PDF for printing?

Drop the book, pick the paper size your printer uses and keep page numbers on. Each chapter starts on a new page, as in a printed book, and images are scaled to the text width. At 11 pt Times our 96,000-word test novel came to 201 A4 pages; 13 pt gives noticeably more pages. To print only some chapters, convert the whole book and then pull out the pages with [Split PDF](/split-pdf).

## Why won't my EPUB convert?

Books bought from Kindle, Apple Books, Kobo, Google Play and most library apps are protected with DRM, which encrypts the chapters. The tool checks for it before anything else and stops with a clear message rather than producing a blank PDF. Removing DRM isn't something this page does.

## When people convert EPUB to PDF

### Printing a public-domain book

Classics from Project Gutenberg or Standard Ebooks print well as PDF: choose Letter or A4, 11 pt Times, and print double-sided.

### Reading on a device that doesn't open EPUB

Some e-readers, work laptops and school tablets open PDF but not EPUB. Convert with Large text for comfortable reading on a small screen, and use [Compress PDF](/compress-pdf) if an illustrated book is too big to email to yourself.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Fix | Why it happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “DRM-protected” message | Use a DRM-free copy of the book. | The chapters are encrypted by the store. |
| “Isn't a valid EPUB” | Download the book again; check it ends in .epub. | The file is damaged or a different e-book format. |
| “?” instead of letters | Use Calibre for books in non-Latin scripts. | The standard PDF fonts cover Latin-script text only. |
| Styling looks plain | Expected; content and structure are what's kept. | The book's CSS and embedded fonts aren't applied. |
| A picture is missing | Check the book opens with the picture in an e-reader. | The image file is missing from the EPUB or in a format the browser can't draw. |
