## How do I resize a photo without losing quality?

Shrinking an image is safe: detail is averaged, not invented. What ruins quality is a single giant downscale step, which leaves jagged edges and shimmering patterns. This tool halves the image repeatedly until it's close to the target, then does the last step with the browser's highest-quality smoothing. The saved JPG or WebP is written at 90% quality by default; raise it to 100% if you'll edit the image again.

Enlarging is different. A 500-pixel image stretched to 2000 pixels has no extra detail to show, so it looks soft. The tool shows a warning before enlarging. For a sharp large print, start from the original photo rather than a downloaded copy.

## How do I resize an image by percentage?

Choose Percent and tap 25%, 50%, 75%, 150% or 200%, or type any value from 1 to 400. Both sides scale by the same amount, so a 4000×3000 photo at 25% becomes 1000×750. In our test that took a 2 MB photo to 179 KB.

## What sizes do social networks and ID photos need?

### Instagram posts and stories

Instagram shows feed photos at up to 1080 pixels wide. Square (1080×1080) and portrait 4:5 (1080×1350) fill the most space in the feed; stories and Reels covers are 1080×1920. Instagram crops anything outside these shapes itself, so cropping here first lets you choose what stays.

### YouTube thumbnails

YouTube recommends 1280×720 and accepts thumbnails up to 2 MB. Use the preset with Crop to fill; if a detailed thumbnail ends up over 2 MB as PNG, save it as JPG.

### Facebook covers, LinkedIn banners and X headers

Covers are wide and short: Facebook 851×315, LinkedIn 1584×396, X 1500×500. Phones crop the sides and profile pictures cover a corner, so keep faces and text near the middle.

### Passport and visa photos (600×600)

The US DS-160 photo must be a square JPEG from 600×600 to 1200×1200 pixels and no larger than 240 KB. The Passport preset makes a 600×600 square crop from the centre; centre your face in the original photo first. Then use [Compress Image](/compress-image) with a custom 230 KB target if the file is still too big.

### Email and websites

For email, 1600–2000 pixels on the long side looks sharp on any screen and keeps each photo a few hundred KB. For a website, resize to the largest size it's shown at (often 1200–1920 px wide) and save as WebP.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Fix | Why it happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Faces look squashed | Choose Crop to fill or Fit inside instead of Stretch. | Stretch forces a new shape. |
| Something important was cropped off | Use Fit inside, or crop the photo yourself first so the subject is centred. | Crop to fill trims equally from both edges. |
| Batch images came out different heights | That's the lock at work: each keeps its shape. Unlock for identical sizes. | Lock gives every image the same width, not the same height. |
| Result looks blurry | Start from a larger original, or don't enlarge past 100%. | Enlarging can't add detail. |
| "Larger than browsers can safely create" | Choose a smaller size (under 8192 px a side, about 16 megapixels). | Browsers, especially on iPhone, refuse huge canvases. |
| HEIC photo rejected | Convert it with HEIC to JPG first. | Most browsers can't decode HEIC directly. |
