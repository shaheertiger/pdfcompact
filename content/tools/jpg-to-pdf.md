## How do I convert JPG to PDF without losing quality?

Keep Image quality on Original. JPG files are copied into the PDF as they are, with no re-compression, so the photos inside the PDF are identical to the files you added. Many converters secretly re-encode every photo, often as PNG, which makes a 3 MB photo into a 15 MB PDF page. This one doesn't: in our test, five images totalling 4,998 KB became a 5,003 KB PDF, about 1 KB of overhead per page.

PNG images are embedded losslessly and keep transparency. WebP, GIF and AVIF can't go into a PDF directly, so they're converted once to high-quality JPEG (92%), or to PNG when they have transparent areas.

## How do I make a PDF from photos smaller?

Choose Balanced or Smaller file before converting. Phone photos are much larger than any page needs: a 12-megapixel photo is about 4000 pixels wide, while an A4 page printed at 300 dpi needs 2480.

| Setting | What happens to each photo | Three 12 MP phone photos (6.3 MB) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Original | Untouched (JPG and PNG) | 6.3 MB PDF |
| Balanced | Scaled to 2480 px, JPEG 82% | 1.25 MB PDF (80% smaller) |
| Smaller file | Scaled to 1600 px, JPEG 70% | 478 KB PDF (93% smaller) |

If a photo is already smaller than the re-saved version, the original is kept, so these settings never make a page bigger. For a PDF you already have, use [Compress PDF](/compress-pdf).

## Can I convert phone photos to PDF on iPhone or Android?

Yes. Open this page in Safari or Chrome, tap the box and choose Photo Library or your gallery. iPhone photos in HEIC format are converted in the browser (Safari reads them natively; other browsers load a decoder the first time, about 1.3 MB). Use the arrows to reorder, since dragging on a small screen is fiddly, then save the PDF to Files or Downloads.

## What page size and orientation should I use?

Auto orientation turns each page to match its photo: landscape photos get landscape pages, portrait photos get portrait pages, all in one PDF. Choose Portrait or Landscape to force every page the same way. Fit to image sizes each page to its photo at 96 pixels per inch, so there are no white borders; this is what most people want for photo albums, while A4 and Letter suit anything that may be printed.

## When people turn photos into a PDF

### CV, ID and certificate scans for job portals

Many applicant tracking systems accept a single PDF of supporting documents, often with a 2–5 MB limit. Photograph your ID card, degree certificate and references, add them here in the order asked for, choose A4 and Balanced, and check the size shown before uploading.

### Receipts for expense claims

Snap each receipt, convert them all into one PDF and attach that to your claim. Small margin and Smaller file keep a month of receipts small enough for email (Gmail's limit is 25 MB per message, Outlook's 20 MB).

### Homework and assignments for school portals

Google Classroom, Canvas and Moodle assignments often want one PDF rather than ten photos. Photograph each page, rotate any that came out sideways and use Balanced so the upload doesn't time out on mobile data.

### Visa and IRCC supporting documents

Immigration portals ask for documents as PDFs with a size cap per file (IRCC's online portal has used 4 MB per upload; check your own checklist). Put bank statements, letters and photos of documents into one PDF per item, then use Balanced or Smaller file to fit the limit.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Fix | Why it happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PDF is too big to upload | Choose Balanced or Smaller file and convert again. | Original keeps full-resolution photos. |
| A page is sideways | Tap the rotate button on that thumbnail. | The photo was taken sideways and had no rotation tag. |
| Pages are in the wrong order | Reorder the thumbnails, then convert again. | Image 1 always becomes page 1. |
| White borders around photos | Set Margin to None, or choose Fit to image. | A4 and Letter pages have a different shape from most photos. |
| "Couldn't read" an image | Open it in a photo app and save it as JPG, then add it again. | The file is damaged or a format this browser can't open. |
| HEIC photos are slow on the first go | Wait for the first photo; the rest are faster. | The HEIC decoder loads once, on demand. |
| Browser tab crashes on a phone | Convert fewer photos at a time, or use Balanced. | Mobile browsers give each tab limited memory. |
