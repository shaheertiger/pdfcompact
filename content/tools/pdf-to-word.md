## How do I convert a PDF to Word without losing formatting?

Some formatting always changes, because a PDF has no paragraphs or styles, only text placed at positions on a page. This converter rebuilds what it can reliably detect. Lines that sit close together with the same text size become one paragraph; a bigger gap, a short last line ending in a full stop, an indent or a bullet starts a new one. Text clearly larger than the body text becomes Heading 1, 2 or 3 by size, so the Word navigation pane works. Font names that say Bold or Italic keep those styles, and text sizes are carried over.

On our 10-page test report the result was 41 headings and 44 body paragraphs. With page breaks on, a paragraph that runs over a page break stays split at the break, like the PDF; untick page breaks and it is joined back into one paragraph (40 paragraphs, one per section, on the same file). Documents with fancy layouts (columns, sidebars, text over images) need more cleanup.

## Can I convert a PDF with tables to Word?

Yes. Before building paragraphs, the converter looks for text arranged in aligned columns, the same detection used by [PDF to Excel](/pdf-to-excel). Each table it finds becomes a real Word table, with the header row in bold, placed where it was on the page. On a 5-page test file with six product tables, all six came out as 3-column Word tables; one whose header row sat alone at the bottom of the previous page got that header as a bold line above it instead. If you only need the numbers, PDF to Excel gives you a spreadsheet instead.

## How do I convert a PDF to Word on my phone?

Open this page in Safari or Chrome, tap the box and pick the PDF from Files, Google Drive or your downloads. The .docx opens in the Word or Google Docs app. Very long or image-heavy PDFs can be slow on older phones; untick “Include images” to speed things up.

## When people convert PDFs to Word

### Updating a CV you only have as a PDF

If the original .docx is lost, convert the PDF, fix the dates and new job, and save it again with [Word to PDF](/word-to-pdf) or Word itself.

### Editing a contract or agreement

Pull the text of a draft agreement into Word, turn on Track Changes and send your edits back, instead of retyping clauses.

### Reusing a report or handout for a class or Canvas course

Take sections from an old PDF report or handout into a new Word document without retyping. Headings arrive as Word headings, so a table of contents works straight away.

### Filling in a form that isn't fillable

When a PDF form has no fields, convert it to Word, type your answers in place and export it back to PDF.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Fix | Why it happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "No selectable text" message | Run the PDF through OCR PDF first, then convert the result. | The pages are images (a scan or photo), so there's no text to rebuild. |
| Columns are interleaved | Copy each column's text separately, or convert one page at a time. | Lines at the same height in two columns are read as one line. |
| Lines broken mid-sentence | Use Word's Find and Replace on paragraph marks, or join the lines by hand. | Uneven line spacing in the PDF looked like a paragraph gap. |
| A table came out as plain lines | Use PDF to Excel for that table and paste it in. | Its columns weren't clearly separated, or it had merged cells. |
| Headings are plain text | Apply Heading styles in Word; they're one click each. | Those headings were the same size as body text in the PDF. |
| A logo or diagram is missing | Take a screenshot and insert it in Word. | It was drawn with vector shapes, not stored as an image. |
| "Password-protected" error | Open it with the password, save an unprotected copy, then convert. | Files locked with an open password can't be read without it. |
